Analysis and discussion

Local scour is the removal of sediment around the bridge pier caused by accelerated water forming vortex patterns when it contacts the pillar. This creates a powerful current that can expose the base foundations and reduce structural stability.

Contractional scour occurs in bridges over rivers with fast-moving water, where the flow narrows from a wide section into a smaller one. This creates increased pressure and can erode the riverbed around the foundations.

Long-term scour is the gradual lowering of riverbeds over time due to natural dynamics, human activities, and stream changes. It slowly reduces the depth of sediment cover and can leave the foundations increasingly vulnerable.

Due to these issues, engineers must design measures to prevent major structural damage. Examples include physical barriers, flow modification techniques, and improved foundation protection.
